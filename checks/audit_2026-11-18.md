# Line-by-line audit, 18 November 2026

## Why

Two earlier reviews read the argument and the wording. Neither recomputed a number from a filing, and an error in a calculation got through: cash profit left out amortization of intangible assets for four companies. It was found only when Oracle's figures were laid out line by line. This audit applied that kind of check to the whole project.

## How

Six separate AI sessions each took one area. Each was told to rebuild its numbers from the printed statements and notes in the filings, without relying on this project's choice of lines, and to report what matched, what did not (with the printed line and link), and every assumption it found. Nothing was changed until each finding had been checked again against the source.

| Area | What was rebuilt |
|---|---|
| The ledger | Each company's borrowings, lease liabilities, cash, twelve-month operating income and depreciation, the lease sentence, the value in today's money and every ratio |
| Cash flows and history | Twelve-month cash figures from the printed cash-flow statements, backlog figures, and 26 quarterly lease figures against their sentences |
| NVIDIA | Every guarantee and commitment, revenue, receivables, days to be paid, fiscal calendar, supply commitments |
| The telecom comparison | All 54 cited filings opened; all 396 amounts checked against their evidence; every ratio rebuilt |
| The fund tool | All nine funds re-parsed from the raw holdings reports with separate code; one fund checked against a second manager's report |
| Appendices and consistency | The equipment-life model rebuilt independently; landlord, other-company and method-check tables sampled; numbers written into fixed text checked against the data |

## What held up

Every figure read directly from a filing matched its source. The arithmetic matched everywhere it was recomputed, including every lease value and every ratio.

## What is now disclosed that was not

- What the 98-cent figure leaves out: Oracle's customer prepayments inside operating cash, and assets taken on under finance leases.
- Money borrowed and shares sold after each quarter ended (listed on each bill, not added in).
- A CoreWeave lease and an Alphabet commitment that sit outside the disclosed totals.
- Meta's one-off legal charge and what its ratio would be without it.
- What the AI hardware list leaves out and how much that would add.
- The model's weakness on Meta in the equipment-life appendix.

## New inputs

`inputs/ledger_notes.csv` holds 47 hand-read figures (recent bond coupons, borrowing after quarter-end, items left out of totals), each with the sentence it came from and a link. `inputs/vendor_finance_2000.csv` gained 64 rows (fourth-quarter sales and Motorola's proxy figures). Every new row's sentence and number were checked against the cited filing twice, by two separate programs.

## What keeps this from happening again

Tests now hold each company's borrowings, lease liabilities, cash, operating income and twelve-month cash figures to the lines printed in its latest report, and the telecom ratios to the independently rebuilt figures. If a company renames a line or the code's choice drifts, the tests fail.

## Not covered

- 88 of the 114 quarterly lease figures (26 were read against their sentences and all matched).
- Landlord-table rows that rest on press reports.
- Whether a newer filing exists than the ones cached on 18 November 2026.
- The audit was done by AI sessions. It is stronger than a read-through and is not the same as a person rebuilding a number by hand; `oracle_by_hand.xlsx` in the owner's notes exists for that.
