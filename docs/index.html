A research note built from SEC filings

AI spending now takes all the cash its biggest builders generate. Nearly a third of an ordinary index fund sits on one side of it or the other.

Six companies now put of every dollar of cash their operations generate into capital spending. What comes on top is paid for with borrowed money, new shares and leases that are not on any balance sheet yet; and for the newest builders, with guarantees from the company that sells the chips. This note follows that money through the filings. Start with your own fund.

What counts, and where this comes from
  • Source. Funds must report every holding to the US securities regulator. The tool reads those reports. They are published a few months late, so the date is shown and today's mix will differ, especially after large price moves.
  • Tickers. The tool reads the holdings of nine funds. Typing a ticker matches it to one of them: the same fund, another fund tracking the same index, or the nearest kind where the note says how yours differs. The list of tickers is mine and covers common US funds, not all of them.
  • The six paying for it are Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Oracle and CoreWeave, among the largest buyers of AI chips and data center space. NVIDIA does not name its customers, so this is my list. Each company is counted whole, although each also has large businesses unrelated to AI.
  • The companies selling the equipment are NVIDIA plus my own list of AI hardware makers: . It leaves out the makers of chip-manufacturing equipment.
  • Sellers and buyers are shown apart on purpose. If spending slowed, the sellers would lose sales while the buyers would keep more of their cash, so the two groups do not carry the same risk.
  • Not advice. The tool shows what is in a fund. It does not say what anyone should hold.

    • The promises

      On top of that, they have signed of leases, mostly for data centers, that are not on their books yet.

      A lease reaches the balance sheet only on the day the landlord hands over the building. Until then it is a single sentence in a footnote. This project collects that sentence from every quarterly report since 2022.

        So a sum larger than everything the six have borrowed is already committed, and none of it counts as debt yet.

        Show the chart and the sources

        Total future payments on leases signed but not yet started, by calendar quarter. These are payments over terms of up to 30 years, not one year's spending. Amazon's figure also covers warehouses and aircraft; Meta's and CoreWeave's include some energy, network and office leases.

        "Worth today" discounts the payments at each company's own lease rate. Those rates were set when borrowing was cheaper, so the true figure is more likely below the middle estimate than above it. Method, range and test →
