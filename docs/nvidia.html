NVIDIA and its customers

A separate question from Lookthrough's main page, built from the same kind of source. The company that sells the chips has begun to stand behind some of the companies building AI data centers. This page sets out what NVIDIA has promised and to whom, how much of it is actually in force, how long it now waits to be paid, and how the same measures looked at the telecom equipment makers of 1997 to 2003.

What NVIDIA has promised, and what is in force

A supplier can support the companies that buy from it in several ways: guaranteeing their debts or leases, promising to invest in them, holding their shares, or agreeing to rent back capacity they build. The chart adds up what NVIDIA discloses, as a share of its current annual rate of sales.

The label above each bar is guarantees plus investment promises only. Guarantees are counted at their maximum. The one drawn in grey is not yet in effect: it starts building by building as each of nine is completed, with the first expected in NVIDIA's 2029 fiscal year (late January 2028 to late January 2029), so the full amount is reached well after that. The July 2025 report did not break out capacity agreements, so the previous quarter's figure is carried forward for that bar and drawn paler. Sales are the quarter's sales times four. One group is not comparable across the last two bars: in July NVIDIA separated $20bn of data center leases it expected to pass to others, which the chart counts as capacity; in October it reported a single $74bn of leases, some of which it says it may assign, and the chart counts none of it.

Getting paid

Days to be paid is what customers owe divided by one day of the quarter's sales. NVIDIA names no customers; it reports only those owing 10% or more of the total, so the last column can fall simply because a customer slipped under 10%.

Then and now: the telecom equipment makers on the same measures

In the late 1990s telecom equipment makers lent money to the phone companies buying their equipment, and many of those customers failed. The comparison with NVIDIA is made often and loosely. Money actually at risk

The definitions are matched as far as the filings allow, but they are not identical. Sales are a rough yardstick for a credit question; they are used because they are the one figure every company here reported on the same basis. NVIDIA's shareholdings are left out, because filings do not separate stakes in customers from other investments. NVIDIA has disclosed no loans to customers.

Limits

  • Every figure on this page comes from a 10-K, 10-Q or 8-K, or for Motorola's guarantees a proxy statement, and is stored with its sentence. The telecom filings predate machine-readable data and were read as text.
  • NVIDIA does not name its customers. Where a counterparty is named here (OpenAI, SB Energy), the filing names it.
  • The latest NVIDIA quarter comes from its results release of 17 November 2026. Figures may be refined when the full quarterly report is filed.
  • The telecom highs are picked with hindsight, and NVIDIA's reading is a single date. The path chart is there so the comparison does not rest on one point.
